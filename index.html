The problem

Your website closes at five. Your clients don't.

Someone has just gone contracting. It's late, they've googled how much tax do I put aside as a sole trader, and they'll choose an accountant in the next fortnight. Right now they land on Hnry, or on a site that says "contact us for a consultation" — and they don't. A brochure can't help anyone at 9pm. A tool can.

The tool

The Set-Aside Calculator

Six questions — income, expenses, other income, GST, kind of work, first year or not — and a real number: how much to put aside each month and from every invoice, whether provisional tax is coming, and when. Then one button: email me the breakdown. It asks for a name and an email. That's your lead, with everything they told it attached.

In September 2026 I swept a national directory of accountants and checked every practice website it listed. Twelve firms had bought the same off-the-shelf pack of twenty generic calculators. Two had built a real one. Not one turned a calculation into a lead. One website in six was dead, parked, or serving casino spam — and the firm probably doesn't know. Nobody is named. The method and the numbers are on the Work page.

That gap is the whole reason Openhours exists: a small practice's website should do what the front desk does — answer the question, take the name — after five.
