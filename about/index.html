I'm Jayden Ashby.

I live in Christchurch, I'm studying — commercial law is where I'm headed — and I build websites and small tools for accounting practices under the name Openhours.

Openhours started with a question that wouldn't go away: why does every small practice's website close at five? Someone googling how much tax should I put aside at nine at night is about to become somebody's client. I checked 23 practice websites in Christchurch, then 160 across the country, to see whose site would answer them. Nobody's did.

So I built the thing that does. One tool, done properly, that sits on the site you already have and turns that question into a name in your inbox.

How I work

Small on purpose. One kind of client — accounting practices — so I actually understand your business, your calendar, and what a lead is worth to you. I write every line myself; you get me, not an account manager. I answer my own email, within one working day.

The name says what the work is for: the useful part of a website is the part that works while you're closed.
