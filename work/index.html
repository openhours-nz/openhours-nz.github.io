What I found · September 2026

160 accounting practice websites. Zero lead capture beyond a contact form.

Method. On 11 September I checked 23 small practices in Christchurch by hand. On 12 September I swept a national directory of accountants — search4accountants.co.nz — across all sixteen regions: 1,766 listings, of which 241 listed a website. A script fetched every homepage and the first linked "calculator" or "tools" page, recorded the platform, forms, widgets and any sign of a tool, and flagged anything broken. Listings that were mortgage brokers, insurers, lenders or software companies were set aside; 160 were accounting or bookkeeping practices. Anything that looked like a real tool was checked by hand.

131

practice websites live and reachable.

26

dead, parked, hijacked or 404. Nineteen of those domains no longer resolve at all — the registration has lapsed. Two now serve casino spam under the firm's old address. One in six.

46

of the 131 live sites have a contact form on the home page — 35%. The rest offer an email address, a phone number, or a "book a consultation" link.

12

have the same off-the-shelf pack of generic calculators — reverse mortgage, loan repayments, "how long to repay". Identical on every site. Built for an Australian audience. None captures a lead.

2

have a genuine custom calculator: one a mileage-claim calculator, one a franchise's GST and rental-yield tools. Neither asks for a name.

0

have a calculator that captures a lead. Not one, in the whole sample.

6

live sites still don't have HTTPS — the browser shows "Not secure" next to the firm's name.

7

have a chat widget or an online booking link, combined. Five and two.

What it means. Almost every practice website in the country is a brochure with a form on it. The only "tools" in circulation are a template pack that answers questions a New Zealand sole trader isn't asking. The one question they are asking — how much should I put aside? — is answered by nobody's website, so it's answered by Hnry's. And one site in six has quietly died under its owner.

Limits. One directory, so firms with no listing there aren't counted. Home pages and one linked page only — a tool buried three clicks deep could be missed. A script can't see everything a browser renders. The counts are conservative in one direction: they can undercount tools, not invent them.

Christchurch subset, checked by hand 11 September: 23 practices, 0 with lead capture, 6 broken. Both samples are kept, row by row, and the numbers on this page will be updated as they grow.
